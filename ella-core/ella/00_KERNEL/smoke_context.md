## Global Workspace Theory (Baars, 1988)

Consciousness is a "global workspace": a large set of unconscious specialist
processors (perception, memory, motor, evaluative) compete for access, and
attention selects winners into the workspace, whose contents are then
broadcast back to the whole system. Unconscious modules are powerful but
isolated; the broadcast is what makes information globally available for
reasoning, reporting and control.
DESIGN LESSON: selection plus global broadcast — let specialists compete,
then broadcast the winners to everyone, instead of letting every module
shout at once.

## Complementary Learning Systems (McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly, 1995)

The brain learns at two speeds: the hippocampus learns fast — one-shot
episodic snapshots — while the neocortex learns slowly, interleaving new
knowledge with old so nothing is overwritten (no catastrophic forgetting).
During sleep, hippocampal traces are replayed to gradually train the cortex.
Fast capture plus slow consolidation is the trick.
DESIGN LESSON: two-speed memory with offline consolidation — capture episodes
fast, consolidate them into structured knowledge slowly, replay during downtime.
